### What happened
The market opened near yesterday's low and rallied hard to about 7,240. The bears then sold it all the way back down to about 7,155. That made a trading range: the opening high at the top, yesterday's low and the opening low at the bottom. In a range, bulls buy low and bears sell high, and most breakouts fail.

I knew the market was in a trading range. I saw a Failed High 2 and sold it anyway, near the bottom of the range. After I got in, the market formed a wedge bottom, three pushes down at support, and I got out at breakeven. The market let me off easy.

Then a sloppy Low 2 formed near the same low, and I sold it. It was the same mistake in the same place. The range held and I was stopped out.

After that, a little angry, I told myself I would take the next signal no matter the context. The next signal was a Failed Low 2, high in the range, just under the opening high. I bought it knowing I was buying the top of a trading range, and I was stopped out. That was enough, and I stopped for the day.

### Review
None of these trades was really my setup. Each one had the right shape in the wrong context. My Failed Low 2 needs a bull trend, and a trading range is not a trend. Selling at the bottom of a range and buying at the top is the opposite of what the market rewards there. Context is king, and I stay out of ranges. Today I knew both and ignored them.

The worst part is that I knew. In the back of my mind I knew it was a risk, and I took the first trade because I wanted it to work. Then the loss turned into anger, and the anger made a rule for me: take any signal. That rule was the real loss today. It was a decision to stop trading my system.

I'm ashamed of today. Two weeks of patience and hard work, and in two days I threw it away. But my system didn't fail today. I did. Those two weeks are the reason I could hear the voice in real time. The next step is acting on that voice before I click, not after.

What I did right: I got out at breakeven when the wedge bottom formed, and I stopped after the third trade instead of trying to win it back.
