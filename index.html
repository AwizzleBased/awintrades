    Learn

    Read the context. Then the setup.

    Before I look for any setup, I read the context. These are the three questions I ask every time.

    Three questions before every trade

    1. 1
      Where are we in the market cycle?

      Breakout, trend, trading range, then a new breakout. Every chart is somewhere in that loop.

    2. 2
      Which side is in control?

      If I had to hold a position right now, long or short? That's the only side I trade.

    3. 3
      Trend or range?

      Overlapping bars, dojis and failed breakouts both ways mean a range. In a range, I don't trade.

    How I Trade

    Simple rules. Every day.

    The same loop every trading day, whether yesterday was green or red.

    • Before 7:15 PTPlan. Key levels, the context, an if/then for both sides.
    • 7:15 PTExecute. Only the plan's setups. Stop at the daily limit.
    • AfterReview. Grade every trade on process, not the result.
    • Same dayJournal. Write it down and note one fix for tomorrow.

    The rules

    • Rule 1Trade with the trend. Only the side the market is already on.
    • Rule 2Read the market cycle. Breakout, trend, range, repeat.
    • Rule 3Context is king. A setup only counts in the right context.
    • Rule 4Stay out of ranges. Sideways, overlapping bars mean no trade.
    • Rule 5One chart. The 5-minute. Price action is fractal.

    Four setups

    TrendPullbackFailed counter-trend
    BullH2 buyFL2 buy
    BearL2 sellFH2 sell

    H2 / L2: the second leg of a pullback ends and the trend resumes. FH2 / FL2: the other side's entry fails, and I trade with the trend against them. These ideas come from Al Brooks' price action; I cover only the parts I use, in my own words.

    • Signal barThe bar that sets up the trade. I wait for it to close before I act.
    • Entry barThe next bar, the one that gets me in. For a buy, it has to trade above the signal bar's high.

    Failed Low 2 FL2 · buy

    A buy in a bull trend. The bears try twice to reverse it, and fail twice.

    • 1 · TrendBull trend. Price shows signs of a bull trend. Always in long.
    • 2 · FL1Failed Low 1. The first attempt down fails.
    • 3 · FL2Failed Low 2. The second attempt fails too. Entry above the signal bar's high.
    • 4 · StopStop. Under the pullback low.

    Why I take it

    In a bull trend the market is always in long. Every pullback is the bears trying to reverse it.

    A Failed Low 2 means the bears tried twice and failed twice. After two failures, most bears give up. They buy back their shorts, and bulls who were waiting buy the pullback. Both sides are buying at the same time.

    That is why I take it. I am not guessing at a reversal. I am trading with the trend, after the other side has shown twice that it cannot stop it. The odds favor the trend continuing.

    About

    Self-taught. One market. Every morning.

    I'm Awin, an ES futures trader based in California. Awin Trades is my public trading journal: every plan, every trade and every review, written down while I work toward consistency.

    I first found trading in high school, watching traders on YouTube. I had no system, only curiosity and one conviction: if other people could learn this, so could I. The deeper I went, the clearer it became that trading is one of the hardest skills to master, and one of the few with no ceiling on how far you can improve. What drew me in was the freedom.

    In 2022 I found Al Brooks, and that's when my real education in price action began. His advice to master a single market stuck with me. I've traded ES ever since, and narrowing my focus to one instrument made me a calmer, less distracted trader. Other markets can come later.

    Today my work is execution: following my rules the same way, every session, and building the consistency that comes from it. The journal is how I measure the process and hold myself to it.

    Freedom comes with a price, and the price is self-discipline.

    Two lessons that shape how I trade

    • LossesLosing trades are the most useful data I have. They show the emotions underneath a decision, and why a setup did or didn't work.
    • RepetitionConsistency means following your rules, every day. It's often boring. That's the point.
